The Stakes

Diné bizaad speakers deserve to be understood — accurately.

Navajo (Diné bizaad) is spoken across the Navajo Nation — spanning Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah — and by Diné families in cities nationwide. Many elders and patients are most comfortable, and most precise, in their own language. A tonal, verb-centered language like Navajo can't be handled by a general bilingual speaker: an inaccurate word in a clinic, courtroom, or classroom can change care, rights, and outcomes.

Honoring the Diné

One of the world's most intricate living languages

The Navajo people call themselves Diné — “the People” — and their language Diné bizaad. Spoken across the Navajo Nation, the largest Native American reservation in the United States, it is among the most widely spoken Indigenous languages in North America — and one of the most complex, with a tonal, verb-centered grammar of remarkable precision.

During World War II, the Navajo Code Talkers built an unbroken code from Diné bizaad that helped secure victory in the Pacific — a lasting testament to the language's depth and power. At the heart of Diné life is hózhó: the philosophy of balance, beauty, and harmony.

We honor that legacy by working only with fluent Navajo linguists who carry both the words and their cultural meaning.
Quality language services support Diné bizaad revitalization for the next generation.
